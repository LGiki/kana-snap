## Design Context

### Users
- **Audience**: All beginners learning Japanese kana, regardless of background — students, hobbyists, self-learners
- **Context**: Study sessions (short or extended), daily practice, reference lookups
- **Job to be done**: Memorize hiragana and katakana efficiently, track progress, stay motivated to keep practicing

### Brand Personality
- **3 words**: Friendly, Focused, Fun
- **Voice**: Encouraging without being patronizing, clear without being dry, celebratory without being overwhelming
- **Emotional goals**: Gamified motivation — users should feel driven, engaged, and rewarded for their progress

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Lively and imaginative — not sterile minimalism, not overwhelming maximalism
- **Inspiration**: Duolingo's motivational systems (streaks, progress, celebration) but NOT its heavy gamification or entertainment-first approach. The app must remain a focused learning tool.
- **Anti-references**: Not cold/corporate, not stiff or boring, not overwhelmingly game-like
- **Theme**: Moving away from single indigo palette — planning multiple user-selectable color theme palettes in settings
- **Light/dark mode**: Both supported

### Design Principles
1. **Motivation through craft, not mechanics** — Use clever micro-interactions, animations, and visual feedback to create motivation rather than relying on heavy game mechanics
2. **Celebrate progress genuinely** — Acknowledge achievements and milestones in ways that feel earned and encouraging, not performative
3. **Focus serves fun** — The interface should feel lively and engaging, but never distract from the core learning task
4. **Personality in the details** — Delight lives in hover states, transitions, success moments, and subtle surprises — not in decorative clutter
5. **Respect the learner** — Treat users as capable adults making an effort to learn, not children who need constant entertainment
